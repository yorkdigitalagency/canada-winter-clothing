What is the best sweater for a Canadian winter?

The best sweater for a Canadian winter is a midlayer, not an outer layer: a moisture-wicking base layer underneath, the sweater for insulation, and a windproof, waterproof parka on top. A sweater alone has no wind or waterproofing, so on its own it cannot handle Canadian winter — but as the middle of the system, it does most of the insulating work.

Wool vs fleece vs synthetic

Wool (especially merino)

Warmest common sweater material, and it keeps insulating even when damp. Heavier and needs more care than fleece. Best for: cold, dry days and anyone who runs cold.

Fleece (synthetic)

Lighter, dries fast, easy to wash, less warm for its weight than wool. Best for: active days, wet coastal winters, and layering under a shell.

Cotton — the one to avoid

Cotton loses warmth when wet and dries slowly. A cotton sweater soaked with sweat or snow stops insulating — the classic cold-weather mistake. Keep cotton for indoors.

What to look for before you buy

  • Fit: room for a base layer underneath, without compressing the insulation. Compressed insulation traps less air and loses warmth.
  • Weight: heavier knit means more warmth, but also more bulk under a parka — match it to your jacket's room.
  • Neck and cuffs: a higher collar and snug cuffs keep drafts out; they matter more than a brand name.
  • Care: if you will wear it daily, easy washing beats delicate wool that needs hand care.
  • Length: a slightly longer hem keeps your lower back covered when you bend and move.

Frequently asked questions

What is the warmest sweater material for winter?

Wool, especially merino wool, is the warmest common sweater material because it insulates even when damp. Fleece is lighter, dries faster and is easier to care for, but is less warm for its weight. Cotton is the material to avoid in winter because it loses warmth when wet and dries slowly.

Should a winter sweater be the outer layer?

No. In Canadian winters a sweater works as a midlayer: a moisture-wicking base layer underneath, the sweater for insulation, and a windproof, waterproof parka on top. A sweater alone has no wind or waterproofing, so it cannot do the outer layer's job.

How should a winter sweater fit?

A winter sweater should fit with room for a base layer underneath and without compressing the insulation. A sweater that is too tight loses warmth because compressed insulation traps less air, and it restricts the movement you need for layering.
